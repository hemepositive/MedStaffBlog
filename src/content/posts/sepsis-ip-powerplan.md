A new **HOSP Adult Sepsis** PowerPlan is now available, giving providers an alternative to Sepsis Advisor and quick access to the latest evidence-based Sepsis Treatment Bundle recommendations, while automating several Sepsis Core Measure (SEP-1) documentation requirements.

<div class="key-message">

This Sepsis Power Plan covers the basic, universal elements of sepsis management — this PowerPlan should not be used as an all-inclusive inpatient order set.

</div>

## Accessing the PowerPlan

Search "sepsis" and select **HOSP Adult Sepsis**.

![Order search screen showing HOSP Adult Sepsis among the results when searching "sepsis"](/images/sepsis-ip-pp/search-sepsis-hosp-adult.png)

The Sepsis Advisor continues to be available and will be updated ASAP.

Fluids, Labs, and Consult selections are visible on the main orderset page. Vasopressors and Antibiotics are available in Subplans.

![Main HOSP Adult Sepsis orderset page showing Continuous Infusions, Medications, Laboratory, and Consults/Referrals sections](/images/sepsis-ip-pp/main-orderset-page.png)

## Main page highlights

**Fluids**

- The 30 mL/kg bolus auto-adjusts volume based on adjusted body weight for high BMI patients, and includes documentation needed to pass SEP-1.
- The Alternative Bolus allows the provider to enter a reduced crystalloid bolus (for any reason) and includes documentation needed to pass SEP-1.

**Vasopressors**

- Takes the provider to the standard (unchanged) ICU Vasopressors and Adjunct Therapy for Shock Subplan.
- Reminder: vasopressor recommendations for sepsis, in order, are Norepinephrine, then add Vasopressin (instead of escalating the Norepinephrine dose), then Epinephrine.

**Laboratory**

- Includes Blood Cultures, Sputum Cultures, and UA with reflex, as well as a nursing communication order to draw cultures before administering antibiotics but not delay antibiotics more than 45 minutes.

**Consults and Referrals**

- Includes ID and Dietician.

## "Special" — Antibiotic Subplans (not pictured)

Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends administering antimicrobials within one hour if shock is present, and three hours if shock is not present.

These sections were written and approved by the Antimicrobial Management Team and represent best practices in most cases. Providers may still need to order medications outside of this PowerPlan to meet individual patient needs, and should reach out to Pharmacy and/or ID with any consultation needs.

### UTI — Urinary Tract Infection

- Default option is Ceftriaxone.
- Zosyn is recommended for Pseudomonas concern; Cefepime is an alternative.
- Ertapenem is the alternative for recent history (90 days) of extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBL) infection.
- Levofloxacin is the alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis).

### CAP — Community Acquired Pneumonia

There are two severity categories (red and green) based on whether the patient has an indication for ICU admission. **Both CAP categories are for patients without MRSA/PSA/Legionella risk factors.** For patients with MRSA/PSA/Legionella risk factors, use the HAP/VAP subplan.

MRSA risk factors primarily include prior lower respiratory MRSA isolation (within 1 year) or hospitalization with IV antibiotics (within 90 days). IDSA offers a [Clinical Pathway for community-acquired pneumonia](https://www.idsociety.org/globalassets/idsa/practice-guidelines/community-acquired-pneumonia-in-adults/cap-clinical-pathway-final-online.pdf).

**Non-severe CAP (not requiring ICU admission)**

- Default option is Ceftriaxone and Azithromycin. Azithromycin is automatically defaulted to 3 days as a P&T approved duration for CAP.
- Doxycycline and Ceftriaxone is the alternative for a patient unable to take Azithromycin (e.g., prolonged QTc interval).
- Levofloxacin is the alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis).

**Severe CAP (requiring ICU admission)** — use HAP/VAP subplan if the patient has MRSA or PSA risk factors

- Default option is Ceftriaxone and Azithromycin.
- Ceftriaxone and Levofloxacin is an alternative.

### HAP/VAP — Hospital-Acquired or Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia

- Default option is Cefepime, which covers risk of Pseudomonas.
- Zosyn is the alternative for anaerobe coverage (e.g., empyema, abscess).
- Levofloxacin is the alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis).
- For MRSA risk, add Vancomycin (first choice) or Linezolid (alternative for renal impairment concerns) in addition to the anti-pseudomonal agent above, and "PCR for nasal MRSA & SA" to allow for possible de-escalation due to high negative predictive value (>95%). MRSA risk factors primarily include prior lower respiratory MRSA isolation (within 1 year) or hospitalization with IV antibiotics (within 90 days).
- For CAP with Legionella risk, add Azithromycin for atypical coverage in addition to the anti-pseudomonal agent above. This is ordered for 5 days and may be extended or discontinued based on Legionella test results and patient presentation.

### SSTI — Skin and Soft Tissue Infection, Cellulitis

- Default is Vancomycin.
- Linezolid is an alternative.

### DFI — Diabetic Foot Infection

- Default is Vancomycin and Ceftriaxone.
- Vancomycin and Cefepime are the alternative if Pseudomonas is a concern.
- Vancomycin and Zosyn are the alternative if Pseudomonas, maceration, and/or anaerobe coverage is a concern.

### Necrotizing Fasciitis

- Default is Vancomycin, Zosyn, and Clindamycin.

### IAI — Intra-Abdominal Infection

- Default option is Ceftriaxone and Metronidazole.
- Cefepime and Metronidazole are the alternatives if Pseudomonas is a concern.
- Ertapenem is the alternative for recent history (90 days) of extended-spectrum beta-lactamase (ESBL) infection.
- Levofloxacin and Metronidazole are the alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis).

### Meningitis/Encephalitis

**Meningitis**

- Default option is Vancomycin and Ceftriaxone.
- Vancomycin and Levofloxacin are the alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies (e.g., anaphylaxis).
- Ampicillin (first choice) or Sulfamethoxazole/Trimethoprim (alternative for severe beta-lactam allergies) should be added for patients over 50, pregnant, or immunocompromised (e.g., history of lymphoma, recent chemotherapy, current high-dose glucocorticoid use or immunosuppressive agents) for Listeria monocytogenes coverage.

**Encephalitis**

- Acyclovir should be added to the above selection for encephalitis coverage (e.g., AMS).

### Febrile Neutropenia

Unchanged existing subplan.

### Source Unknown

- Default option is Vancomycin and Cefepime.
- Vancomycin and Zosyn are the alternative for anaerobe coverage.
