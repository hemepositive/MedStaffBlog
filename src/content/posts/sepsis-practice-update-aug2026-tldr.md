<div class="key-message">

Sepsis is often treated before diagnostic certainty exists. Waiting for all criteria to be met may delay life-saving therapy — clinical judgment remains our most important diagnostic tool.

This is a condensed synopsis. For the full clinical practice update with detailed evidence, data, and citations, see [Early Recognition and Management of Sepsis](/sepsis-practice-update-aug2026).

## Suspect sepsis early

Consider sepsis whenever a suspected or confirmed infection is accompanied by **new organ dysfunction**: hypotension or new vasopressor need, altered mental status, new/worsening oliguria or rising creatinine, elevated lactate, rising bilirubin, hypoxemia, or new thrombocytopenia/coagulopathy. A low threshold for evaluation and treatment is encouraged.

## Act on these priorities

- **Cultures first, without delaying treatment.** Draw blood cultures as soon as possible, ideally before antimicrobials.
- **Antibiotic timing depends on shock and diagnostic certainty:**
  - **Within 1 hour** — possible/probable/definite septic shock, or probable/definite sepsis without shock.
  - **Within 3 hours** — possible sepsis without shock, after a rapid time-limited assessment.
- **Check lactate early and serially.** A lactate >2 mmol/L raises concern for hypoperfusion and should prompt reassessment of resuscitation.
- **Give at least 30 mL/kg IV balanced crystalloid within 3 hours** for sepsis-associated hypoperfusion (actual body weight if BMI <30; adjusted/ideal if BMI ≥30). Treat this as a starting target, not a ceiling — reassess frequently to avoid under- or over-resuscitation.
- **Don't reflexively reduce the fluid target for cardiac or kidney comorbidities.** Recent data show ≥30 mL/kg is associated with lower 30-day mortality even in patients with severe cardiac or kidney disease. Individualize with reassessment, but comorbidities alone shouldn't trigger a reduced target. [Comorbidities, Weight-Based Initial Fluid Resuscitation, and Mortality in Patients With Sepsis.](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.18232)
- **Use the Sepsis PowerPlan** whenever clinically appropriate to streamline management and bundle compliance.

## Document clearly

- CMS presumes sepsis unless documented otherwise — if organ dysfunction is unrelated to infection, document the alternative diagnosis.
- If the full fluid volume isn't clinically indicated, briefly document why (e.g., "concern for fluid overload").

<div class="takeaway">

**Recognize early. Treat promptly. Reassess frequently. Document clearly.**
