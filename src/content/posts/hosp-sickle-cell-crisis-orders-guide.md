The tip sheets and PCA instructions for managing adult inpatients in acute sickle cell pain crisis, ordered via the **HOSP Sickle Cell Crisis Orders** power plan, have been consolidated into one reference guide, now available under **Resources** in the site menu.

## Why this exists

Guidance for sickle cell pain crisis admissions was previously spread across separate tip sheets, which made it hard to answer a bedside question quickly — particularly around PCA starting doses and when transfusion is actually indicated. The new guide brings it together for hospitalists, advanced practice providers, and pharmacy staff, with a quick-reference summary table at the end.

## What's in it

- **Foundational principles** — analgesia within 60 minutes, checking for an Individualized Care Plan before ordering, continuing home medications, and targeting euvolemia rather than aggressive hydration.
- **Multimodal non-opioid analgesia** — why acetaminophen and ibuprofen/naproxen should be *scheduled rather than PRN* for the first 5–7 days, why ibuprofen and naproxen are preferred over ketorolac, and how to assess renal function first.
- **PCA dosing** — starting regimens for opioid-naïve and opioid-tolerant adults, contraindications, and why routine basal rates are not recommended in adults.
- **Monitoring and titration** — what to review in the PCA record, how to escalate for inadequate relief, and what to do for over-sedation.
- **Transfusion thresholds** — the indications that justify transfusion beyond the pain crisis itself, and the 10 g/dL hemoglobin ceiling.
- **Transition to oral opioids** — the 24-hour rule and how to wean a basal rate.

<div class="takeaway">

Treat the pain within an hour, follow the Individualized Care Plan if one exists, schedule non-opioid analgesia rather than ordering it PRN, start with PCA instead of intermittent IV pushes, and reserve transfusion for complications beyond the pain crisis itself.

</div>

Questions about PCA initiation, titration, or the transition to oral opioids should go to pharmacy early in the admission.
