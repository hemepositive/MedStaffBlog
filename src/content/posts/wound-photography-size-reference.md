When you photograph a wound, include a reference object in the frame so size can be judged from the image itself. A sterile 2x2 or 4x4 gauze works well and is already at the bedside. A disposable paper measuring guide or adhesive photo scale is even better, since it gives actual centimeter markings.

<div class="key-message">

Including a scale with wound imaging is best practice, reflected in wound care society and institutional photography guidelines, and many facilities make it a local policy requirement. Photography augments written documentation — it does not replace measuring and charting length, width, depth, and tissue characteristics.

</div>

## Why it matters

- A photo without a reference has no scale. Camera distance and angle change apparent size, so two images of the same wound can look very different.
- With a known-size object in frame, anyone reviewing the chart — consultant, wound care, utilization review, the next shift — can see whether the wound is larger or smaller than last time.
- It supports the measurements you document and strengthens the record if the wound is later reviewed for quality, litigation, or reportable-event purposes.
