<div class="key-message">

Do **not** clamp short-term indwelling urethral catheters (≤7 days) before removal. The practice offers no benefit and is associated with harm. Remove catheters promptly once no longer indicated.

</div>

## Why clamping is not recommended

- A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies (3,908 participants) found clamping increased the risk of urinary tract infection (RR 1.47; 95% CI 1.26–1.72) and prolonged the time to first void compared with free drainage. The UTI signal was stronger in the ≤7-day subgroup (RR 1.69; 95% CI 1.42–2.02) (Ma, Gu, and Fan, *BMJ Open*, 2023).
- The 2021 Cochrane review found clamping made little to no difference in recatheterization risk (RR 0.82; 95% CI 0.55–1.21; low-certainty) and provided insufficient evidence of any effect on urinary retention. The review also cites CDC guidance concluding there is no benefit to clamping short-term catheters (Ellahi et al., *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews*, 2021).
- A 2019 randomized controlled trial in 218 total hip/knee arthroplasty patients found no advantage of clamping over free drainage for recatheterization due to retention (2.6% vs 5.8%; p=0.316), concluding the procedure is not indicated (Markopoulos et al., *International Journal of Nursing Studies*, 2019).

## Caveats

- Evidence for catheters in place &gt;7 days is insufficient; the meta-analysis could not draw firm conclusions for longer durations (Ma, Gu, and Fan, *BMJ Open*, 2023).
- A small number of individual studies (e.g., a post-TURP trial) reported improved storage symptoms or voiding volumes with clamping, so the literature is not entirely uniform — but pooled and guideline-level evidence weighs clearly against routine clamping (Büyükyilmaz et al., *Journal of Clinical Nursing*, 2020).

## Recommended practice

- Remove the catheter as soon as it is no longer needed; use passive balloon deflation (Fletke, Jeong, and Herrera, *American Family Physician*, 2024).
- Perform a voiding trial: expect a spontaneous void within ~6 hours and assess post-void residual by bladder ultrasound to detect retention (Fletke, Jeong, and Herrera, *American Family Physician*, 2024).
- In men, consider tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily (started at insertion, ≥3 days) to improve trial-of-void success (Cameron and Werneburg, *JAMA Surgery*, 2025).
- Post-removal measures such as scheduled/delayed voiding to rebuild bladder capacity and pelvic floor exercises (for long-term catheter users) may be useful — unlike pre-removal clamping (Fletke, Jeong, and Herrera, *American Family Physician*, 2024).

<div class="takeaway">

**Skip the clamp.** Remove short-term Foley catheters promptly with passive balloon deflation, then confirm bladder function with a voiding trial rather than a pre-removal clamping protocol.

</div>
