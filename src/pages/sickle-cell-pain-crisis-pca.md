# Patient-Controlled Analgesia for Sickle Cell Pain Crisis

_Part 2 of the [HOSP Sickle Cell Crisis Orders Guide](/sickle-cell-pain-crisis) — dosing, monitoring, and titration for opioid analgesia via PCA._

<div class="key-message">

For fluids, non-opioid analgesia, transfusion criteria, and the rest of the pain crisis pathway, see the [main HOSP Sickle Cell Crisis Orders guide](/sickle-cell-pain-crisis). For a walkthrough of where these choices live in the order set itself, see the [order-set click-through guide](/sickle-cell-pain-crisis-order-set-guide).

</div>

PCA uses a computerized pump to deliver IV analgesia on the patient's own schedule. Compared with intermittent IV boluses, PCA shortens time-to-treatment; compared with continuous infusion, PCA achieves similar pain control with lower cumulative opioid exposure. Early, hospitalist-led PCA initiation has been associated with reduced length of stay.

**Prefer PCA over intermittent IV pushes.** Start with PCA, then transition to oral medication — do not "bridge" a patient who has already transitioned to oral meds with intermittent IV pushes. If pain becomes uncontrolled, restart the PCA instead.

![PCA demand dosing vs. IV push: timing to peak effect and duration of analgesia](/images/sickle-cell-pain-crisis/pca-vs-iv-push.jpg)

## PCA components

- **Demand dose** — an opioid bolus delivered on patient request through a button, subject to a fixed lockout interval.
- **Lockout interval** — minimum time between demand doses; must be long enough for the prior dose to take effect.
- **Basal rate** — a continuous background infusion independent of demand dosing.

## Absolute contraindications to PCA

- Patient unable to understand the PCA concept.
- Decline in mental status with impending risk of airway compromise.
- Allergy to the PCA-delivered opioid.
- Infection at the PCA access site.

## Basal/continuous rates in adults

Routine use of basal continuous infusion is **not recommended** in adults and should be reserved for opioid-tolerant patients who cannot tolerate oral home opioid medication or those with a specific Individualized Care Plan (ICP) indication. Basal rates increase the risk of nocturnal over-sedation and respiratory depression. Continuous pulse oximetry is pre-selected in the PCA orders.

Opioid tolerance is defined in the **HOSP Sickle Cell Crisis Orders** power plan as **≥ 60 mg oral morphine equivalents per day**.

### Opioid tolerance

The FDA defines **opioid tolerance** as receiving **≥60 mg oral morphine per day** (or equianalgesic equivalent) for one week or longer. ## PCA order set — fixed dosing by drug and tolerance status

The **HOSP Sickle Cell Crisis Orders** power plan does not offer a provider-adjustable dosing range; each order fires with a fixed demand dose, lockout, 4 hour limit, and (where applicable) continuous rate. Morphine and Dilaudid (hydromorphone) PCA orders are presented as parallel options in the order set — selection is driven by clinical judgment/allergy history, not a first-line/second-line hierarchy.

| Parameter               | Morphine PCA — not tolerant | Morphine PCA — tolerant | Dilaudid PCA — not tolerant | Dilaudid PCA — tolerant |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Demand dose             | 1 mg                        | 1 mg                    | 0.2 mg                      | 0.2 mg                  |
| Lockout                 | 10 min                      | 10 min                  | 10 min                      | 10 min                  |
| 4-hour limit            | 24 mg                       | 24 mg                   | 4.8 mg                      | 4.8 mg                  |
| Continuous (basal) rate | 0 mg/hr (none)              | 0 mg/hr                 | 0 mg/hr (none)              | 0 mg/hr                 |

**Breakthrough dosing while on PCA.** Each PCA order pairs with a fixed IV push order for severe breakthrough pain (score 8–10): morphine PCA pairs with morphine 2 mg IV push q5min PRN, not to exceed 10 mg every 2 hours; Dilaudid PCA pairs with Dilaudid 0.3 mg IV push q5min PRN, not to exceed 2 mg every 2 hours. This breakthrough order is meant to run alongside an active PCA — it is not the same as the intermittent IV pushes this guide advises against using to bridge a patient already transitioned to oral medication.

## Starting regimens

- Review the patient's home opioid regimen and ideally the regimen used at prior hospitalizations.
- Consider initiating at the PCA dose used during the most recent prior hospitalization.
- PCA dosing is the same regardless of home opiate dosing: discuss starting a low continuous rate for opioid-tolerant patients who cannot take oral medications.
- Engage pharmacy early for guidance and questionsx with PCA initiation, titration, and the transition to oral pain medication.

Useful calculators: [MDCalc Morphine Milligram Equivalents](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/10170/morphine-milligram-equivalents-mme-calculator) and [StatPearls, "Opioid Equivalency"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK441913/). Note that hydromorphone and morphine potency differ substantially — 1 mg IV hydromorphone ≈ 6.6 mg IV morphine; 0.4 mg IV hydromorphone ≈ 2.6 mg IV morphine. **Confirm any conversion with pharmacy.**

## Continuous monitoring and dose titration

Review PCA use history and the nursing record regularly: number of attempted actuations (button presses, including those blocked by lockout), number of delivered doses, and total medication administered. Combine numeric pain scores with **functional** pain assessment — ability to cough, take a deep breath, and rest comfortably.

### How to check PCA use history

You can query the pump directly by going through its menus to see how many demand doses the patient requested versus how many were actually delivered. The nurses can show you how to navigate those menus. This information is also generally documented in the nursing record at the end of every shift. Here is a video if you want to check the PCA use yourself:
[Viewing PCA history and monitor trending with Alaris™ System](https://www.bd.com/en-us/videos/viewing-pca-history-and-monitor-trending-with-alaris-system)

### Adverse effects to monitor

- **Over-sedation / respiratory depression** — consider continuous end-tidal CO₂ monitoring for patients on high-dose PCA (per order set); the Narcan/naloxone order is 0.1 mg IV push q1min PRN for RR < 7, apnea, or over-sedation (POSS = 4), max 0.4 mg.
- **Constipation** — scheduled stimulant laxative plus osmotic stool softener (pre-checked by default in the order set).
- Urinary retention, pruritus/itching, hypotension, insomnia, nausea/vomiting.

### If over-sedated or showing respiratory depression

- Hold all opioids until mental status improves.
- Consider naloxone.
- Optimize alternative non-opioid therapies (heat, acetaminophen, methocarbamol, temporary ibuprofen).
- If continued PCA use is required, lower bolus/basal rates and/or lengthen the lockout interval.
- Order End-tidal CO2 monitor if not alread ordered.

### If pain relief is inadequate

- **Repeated end-of-lockout activations with otherwise adequate relief** → shorten the lockout interval (e.g., 10 min → 8 min).
- **Consistent repeated actuations without relief** → escalate the demand dose by 50% for somewhat unrelieved pain, or 100% for completely uncontrolled pain (e.g., hydromorphone 0.4 mg → 0.6 mg).
- **No relief despite significant dose/interval adjustment** → optimize non-opioid adjuncts, rotate to a different opioid at an equipotent dose, and evaluate for an alternative pain etiology (e.g., infection, compartment syndrome).

## Transitioning from PCA to oral opioids

- Discuss the plan to transition to oral opioids with the patient **early**, before it happens.
- **24-hour rule:** do not transition to oral medication until the patient has had stable pain control with minimal demand-dose activations for 24 hours.
- If a basal rate is running, decrease or stop it, but continue bolus (demand) dosing for 24 hours.
- Assess total opioid equivalents used over the prior 24 hours and convert to an oral opioid–equivalent dose (pharmacy can assist).
- Schedule the oral opioid regimen once the basal rate is stopped:
  - _Non-opioid-tolerant patients_ — consider transitioning to a home opioid regimen.
  - _Opioid-tolerant patients_ — continue the home regimen with adjustments as needed.
- Monitor for opioid withdrawal (sweating, diarrhea, agitation, tachycardia, nasal congestion, piloerection) and adjust dosing if it occurs.
- Do not bridge with intermittent IV pushes once a patient is on oral medication — return to PCA if pain becomes uncontrolled.

<div class="takeaway">

Prefer PCA over intermittent IV pushes. Use a basal rate only for opioid-tolerant patients or per ICP. Monitor pump history and functional pain status, not just numeric scores. Don't transition to oral until 24 hours of stable control, and don't bridge with IV pushes afterward.

</div>
